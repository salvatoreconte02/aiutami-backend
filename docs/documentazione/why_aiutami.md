

The problem  ￼

Group conversations are messy. People talk over each other, some dominate while others stay silent, and discussions often lose focus. Important insights get buried, participation becomes uneven, and the group struggles to reach clear decisions. At the same time, current LLMs are not reliable in multi-turn conversations, so they can’t provide real-time support in these situations. This creates a real need for tools that bring structure, fairness, and clarity to group discussions.

⸻

UNG Model  ￼

Our target users are small collaborative groups —students, coworkers, or teams who need to discuss and make decisions together. They need clarity, fair turn-taking, and help keeping the conversation organized. Facilitators and teachers also need support in monitoring participation and guiding the flow. The goal of our system is to provide an AI moderator that structures the discussion, balances participation, and helps groups reach clear, shared outcomes.

⸻

The Solution  ￼

Our solution is AIutami, an AI moderator that helps groups have clearer and more organized conversations. It manages turn-taking so only one person speaks at a time, keeps track of what has been said through short summaries, and gently guides the discussion when it becomes stuck, unbalanced, or unfocused. At a high level, AIutami acts like a neutral facilitator: it listens, understands the flow, and intervenes only when helpful—ensuring that everyone can participate and that the group moves toward a shared outcome. What makes it innovative is the combination of real-time voice interaction and intelligent moderation, bringing structure and clarity to group conversations in a way that current tools do not.

⸻

Impact and Applications  ￼

AIutami improves group discussions by making them clearer, fairer and more focused. Users no longer have to compete to speak or worry about missing important points—the system structures the flow, balances participation, and keeps everyone aligned. A key scenario we are developing is the Murder Mystery activity. Here, participants must share clues and collaborate to reach a final verdict, but discussions often become messy or dominated by a few voices. With AIutami managing turns and summarizing the conversation, the group stays organized and can reason together more effectively. The added value is simple: better structure leads to better collaboration and better decisions. And the same benefits extend to study groups, workshops, and any context where people need to talk and decide together.
